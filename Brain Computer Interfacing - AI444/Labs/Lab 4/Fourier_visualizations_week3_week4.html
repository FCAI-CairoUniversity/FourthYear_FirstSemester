Week 3: Waves and the Fourier Transform
1.From a spinning arrow to a wave

From a spinning arrow to a wave

The arrow spins around the circle. Its shadow on the x-axis is cos, its shadow on the y-axis is sin. Recording the shadows over time draws the waves.

A · cos(2π f t + φ)

The dashed lines connect the arrow's shadows to the newest point of each wave. Try φ = −90°: the cosine becomes a sine.

2.Phase difference: two waves with the same frequency

Two waves, same frequency

Adding two waves = adding their arrows tip to tail. They spin together, so the angle between them (Δφ) never changes. The sum is one wave with the same frequency.

Try 0° (reinforce), 180° (cancel), 90° (√2).

A_result = √(A₁² + A₂² + 2·A₁·A₂·cos Δφ): the length of the green arrow. Perfect cancellation needs A₁ = A₂ and Δφ = 180°.

Build a signal, find its waves

1 s of signal, 100 samples. The default is the Week 3 test signal 1 + 2·sin(2π·4t) + cos(2π·10t + π/4).

Wrap the signal at a test frequency

Bottom: multiplying the signal by an arrow spinning backwards at the test frequency wraps it around the origin. The average point (red) is the Fourier coefficient: it is far from 0 only when the test frequency is really in the signal. Hover the spectrum to read amplitude and phase.

Week 4: The Inverse Fourier Transform and Negative Frequencies
4.The inverse transform: arrows draw the signal

Inverse transform: arrows draw the signal

Every Fourier coefficient is an arrow. Let each one spin at its own frequency and put them tip to tail: the last tip draws the signal (the signal comes from part 3 above).

Here the real part is drawn upwards, so the tip lines up with the time plot.

Few coefficients = compression. Add noise in part 3 and keep 5: the noise disappears (denoising).

Gray dots: the original samples. Cyan: the signal rebuilt from the kept arrows. Red: the current tip.

5.Negative frequencies

Negative frequencies

A real cosine is made of two half-size arrows spinning in opposite directions (+f and −f). Their up/down parts cancel, so the sum stays on the real axis.

A complex signal A·(cos + i·sin) is one arrow: no mirror in the spectrum.

Animation slowed down 10×. Spectrum: index fi from 0 to 99; indices above N/2 = 50 are the negative frequencies.
